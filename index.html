In the autumn of 2024, I left the place where I was born and raised.

In the months that followed, the ordinary routes of daily commutes and the quiet corners found during weekend wanderings became new landscapes to me. I began to notice how light shifted across streets I had never walked before, how strangers carried fragments of untold stories in their gestures, and how each fleeting scene could hold a trace of time.

Photography became my way of gathering these fragments — stitching together moments so they would not dissolve into forgetfulness. By arranging them within a narrative, I seek to explore how stories, even the smallest ones, can reshape the way we see our everyday lives. Each image is not only a record, but also an unanswered question: What changes when we choose to look closely?

Nadar Lau
